\chapter*{ABSTRACT}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Interpersonal persuasion in multi-turn natural language dialogue is characterized by fundamental communicative asymmetry: one participant acts as an arguer employing rhetorical tactics to induce compliance, while the counterparty acts as a decider evaluating concessions and formulating commitments. Standard computational models frequently treat multi-turn dialogues as symmetric or monolithic sequences, discarding functional role boundaries, suffering from tokenizer truncation, and exploiting superficial lexical heuristics in closing turns. This capstone project presents an end-to-end computational framework for modeling conversational intention and persuasion strategies across asymmetric dialogue domains.

First, we address conversational intention prediction within the \textit{Persuasion for Good} (P4G) charity solicitation corpus. Demonstrating that recorded transaction metadata severely diverges from grounded textual commitments, we conduct three successive human annotation passes, refining the target into a strict unconditional binary commitment formulation ($N=1{,}017$, 694 yes / 323 no). We document the systematic 14-version evolutionary trajectory of neural intention architectures across four stages. Early flat sequence encoders ($256$ tokens) suffer from early-window truncation and metric collapse ($\text{Macro-F1} \approx 0.65$). Hierarchical dialogue transformers ($2{,}048$-token capacity) overcome the length bottleneck, achieving $0.793$--$0.834$ Macro-F1. To explicitly reflect conversational asymmetry, we design the \textit{AntiphonyClassifier} (v14): a speaker-disentangled, dual-stream architecture with directional cross-attention (where the decider stream queries the persuader stream) and a recurrent commitment-trajectory GRU read at the true final decider turn. Antiphony establishes project-peak test Macro-F1 scores of $0.8623$ (DeBERTa-v3-base, default threshold) and $0.8600$ (tuned threshold) on the second-pass labels, and $0.8446$ (RoBERTa-base, tuned threshold) on the strict third-pass labels.

Second, we model persuasion strategies across $10{,}600$ Persuader turns under an $11$-category, $41$-strategy hierarchical taxonomy. Using a contextual batching pipeline with a four-tier decision protocol and algorithmic validation, we establish an empirical corpus with $48.9\%$ multi-label prevalence ($1.679$ labels per turn). Through near-duplicate text similarity analysis across distinct annotators, we derive an empirical upper performance ceiling of $0.79$ micro-F1. We formulate a multi-task category-first modeling architecture leveraging an ensemble pool of 19 diverse members (fine-tuned transformers, few-shot representations, TF-IDF baselines, and zero-shot cues) trained under Asymmetric Loss. Systematic evaluation of decision combiners demonstrates that a calibrated power-mean blend ($q=+2.0$) achieves superior performance (v18 headline val Macro-F1 $0.5597$, micro-F1 $0.6949$; v17 micro-optimal point $0.7062$ micro-F1, $0.5642$ macro-F1), while an XGBoost stacking layer was systematically tuned but failed to surpass the calibrated blend ($0.5508$ vs $0.5716$).

Third, we investigate cross-domain generalization to conversational e-commerce negotiation by constructing a 700-dialogue benchmark through persona-conditioned LLM synthesis, organic humanization with controlled linguistic noise, and a $100\%$ manual audit. Input ablations reveal that monolithic sequence models rely on distributed merchant concessions rather than decider trajectory tracking. On an adversarially augmented corpus ($N=700$) containing non-monotonic stance reversals, the localized closing-turn control degrades to $0.9235$ Macro-F1, while Antiphony maintains $0.9619$ Macro-F1, widening the sufficiency gap from $+0.0001$ to $+0.0384$ ($+3.84\%$). Finally, forensic post-mortems resolve subtle tokenization artifacts, proving that left-truncation recovers up to $+0.1198$ Macro-F1 obscured by default right-truncation.

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Persuasion Strategy, Conversational Intention, Asymmetric Dialogue, Antiphony Architecture, Multi-Label Classification, Adversarial Reversal.
